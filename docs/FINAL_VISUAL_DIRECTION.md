# Final visual direction — Systems with a pulse

Status: **LOCKED FOR IMPLEMENTATION**
Date: 2026-08-29

## Creative thesis

Omar Khair works between worlds: engineering and image, business systems and
human behaviour, Arabic product culture and global product craft, implementation
and launch. The portfolio should not flatten that range into a services page.

The authored idea is:

> **SYSTEMS WITH A PULSE**

The site begins as a rigorous coordinate system and gradually becomes more
human, tactile, colourful and cinematic as the work appears. Structure is the
frame; the projects provide the emotion.

## North star

**Editorial precision × product evidence × controlled creative motion**

Every creative decision must satisfy at least one of these:

1. make Omar's point of view more recognisable;
2. make real project evidence easier to understand;
3. improve narrative rhythm or spatial orientation.

If an effect satisfies none of them, it does not belong.

## First five seconds

- Omar Khair is immediately identifiable.
- The phrase "Systems with a pulse" is the dominant memory.
- The interface feels authored before it moves: oversized type, a strict
  twelve-column grid, intentional negative space and one living signal field.
- No pricing, stack dump, card grid or generic call-to-action competes with the
  identity.

## Visual grammar

- Foundation: ink black and warm paper, not SaaS white and grey.
- Accent: signal orange for navigation, coordinates and active states.
- Project chapters own their colour rather than being forced into one palette.
- Typography: Manrope for exact information and Instrument Serif for human
  interruptions, quotes and contrast.
- Grid: twelve columns on desktop, four on mobile. Elements may break the grid
  deliberately, never accidentally.
- Image treatment: product screens are source material for scale, crops,
  masks, sequences and contrast. They are never tiny decoration.
- Geometry: sharp editorial frames and soft image masks coexist. Repeated
  rounded card containers are not the default composition.

## Signature interaction

The hero contains a PixiJS signal field derived from the page grid. Points form
connections, drift and respond to the pointer: a technical system behaving like
a living thing. It is atmosphere, not a game, and must disappear cleanly under
reduced-motion or low-power conditions.

The motion system is completed by:

- an editorial route shutter;
- chapter counters and scroll cadence;
- restrained image parallax and mask reveals;
- typographic entrances that preserve reading order;
- Lenis only on devices that have not requested reduced motion.

Three.js is intentionally deferred. A 3D scene currently adds weight without
strengthening the thesis. WebGL is already used meaningfully through PixiJS.

## Reference synthesis

The implementation borrows principles, not layouts:

- Artem Shcherban: clear content architecture and typographic depth.
- Jonas Reymondin: structured layouts with one geeky interaction language.
- Kristian Ulrych: focused case-study hierarchy and smooth transitions.
- Benzo the Great Developer: proof that a strong personal world can coexist
  with GSAP/PixiJS, without borrowing the theme.
- Code by Jesse: responsive motion serving a creative-developer identity.
- Camila Rosa: an artist's work remains the colour and emotional centre.

## Information architecture

- Home is an opening sequence and a curated argument, not a complete archive.
- Work is organised into Flagship Systems, Client & Business Work, Creative
  Direction and Independent Work.
- Flagship case studies get project-specific colour and rhythm within a shared
  navigation system.
- About frames the combination: Product + Engineering + Visual Direction +
  Launch.
- Commercial packages and the 1,000 EGP offer remain useful but live below the
  identity layer, primarily on their own page.

## Stop conditions

The direction is not complete merely because the build is green. It must also
pass desktop and mobile screenshot review, keyboard/focus review, reduced-motion
review, overflow checks, real-image checks and a manual first-five-seconds
comparison against the reference standard.
